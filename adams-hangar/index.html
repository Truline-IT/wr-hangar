How War Robots plays

War Robots is a team based, third person mech shooter made by Pixonic and published by MY.GAMES. It launched on iOS in April 2014, reached Android in 2015 and PC in 2018. You do not bring one robot to a match. You bring a whole hangar, and every time one is destroyed you drop the next one into the fight.

Official Pixonic or MY.GAMES posts Community fan wiki or forum This account seen in Moody.Adam's own game Model my reading, not Pixonic's code

The core loop

  1. BuildFill hangar slots with robots, weapons, pilots, drones and specializations. Pick a titan.
  2. QueueMatchmaking puts together two teams and drops you on a map.
  3. FightDeploy, hold beacons, trade robots. Lose one, drop the next.
  4. EarnSilver, gold, platinum, components, Intel, task and event progress.
  5. UpgradeSpend it on levels and MK upgrades, then go back to step one.

What fills a hangar slot

Every slot is the same equation: a chassis, the guns it can carry, and three layers of bonus on top.

Robot

The chassis. It sets durability (health), speed, hardpoints (Heavy, Medium, Light) and one ability on a cooldown, such as a teleport, stealth or a shield.

Yours: Void Shoggoth is 2 Heavy + 2 Medium. Frozen Dagon is 6 Light.

Weapons

Each fits one hardpoint size. They differ in damage, range, reload style (magazine, auto recharge, overheat) and the effect they apply, such as Rust or Freeze.

Yours: Kirin is Heavy, 350 m, auto recharge, applies Rust and Crumble.

Pilot

A character with skills that boost the robot or one weapon family. Robot pilots unlock a new skill slot each time they are promoted after level 10. Community

Yours: Otto Schreyer gives +35% fire rate to radiation weapons.

Drone

A small companion that rides with the robot. Most add a passive bonus and some fire their own gun or apply an effect.

Yours: Kestrel stacks Deathmark up to 30% and heals on entering stealth.

Specialization

Rows of hexagon cells on robots above Tier 1. They replaced the old module system from 10.5.2. The Basic row unlocks the Active Module slot. Most robots also get three Additional rows, and you pick one to be active. Official

Titan

A giant robot that charges during the match and drops in once. It uses Alpha and Beta weapons, its own titan pilots and titan specializations. Titans cannot use robot pilots or drones. Community

Yours: Atlas level 23 with Void 10 + Chasm 10 x2.

Defence Points (DP)

DP is a flat armor stat that cuts every hit by a percentage. The curve flattens as you add more, so you can never reach full immunity.

damage blocked = DP / (100 + DP) Community wiki formula, confirmed by a 2019 forum test. A simpler way to read it: each DP is about +1% effective durability, so 100 DP doubles how much damage a robot can take. Community

Defense Mitigation is the counter. Some weapons ignore part of the target's DP, like Gauss and titan Beta weapons. Others ignore all of it, like Reaper and maxed titan Alpha weapons. A robot that relies on DP melts against these. Community

Where your DP comes from. Ammit turns damage absorbed by its frontal shield into DP for the rest of the match (11.7, January 2026). Its drone Scales can grant immunity to Defense Mitigation. Shai drone stacks DP as you deal damage, 12 per stack since 11.2. Khepri's Bond gives linked allies +40 DP. Freezo drone gives 95 DP after effects.

Workshop and components

How it works now

Since 11.0: up to 3 production slots, each runs a 24 hour cycle for any component type, and production is free. Official

You can collect what has built up at any time. After 24 hours the slot stops and you start a new task. The ad button adds bonus components without touching the running job.

Why components matter

Robots and weapons are often unlocked from pieces instead of bought whole. Each item needs a set number of components, shown inside the Workshop.

A slot left idle is lost progress, so the habit is: collect and restart once a day.

The older versions

Workshop 2.0 (2018) charged silver, grew to 6 slots with Workshop level and let gold speed things up. It replaced Workshop Points, which were turned into Black Market Keys at 5 points per key. Official

Intel

Intel is a permanent, account wide bonus against specific T4 items. Added in 10.3. At the end of each match you get Intel on every T4 robot, titan and weapon that you and your team destroyed. Official

Against a robot, Intel means you deal more damage to it with every weapon. Against a weapon, it means you take less damage from it. Destroy 100 Ravagers and every gun you own hits Ravagers harder.

Shooting a weapon off a robot with a critical hit gives no Intel for that weapon. Track progress in Collections: tap an item to see your Intel on it and the bonus it gives.

Pixonic has not published the bonus at each step. One 2024 forum report says about 30% Intel gave 5% more damage, 5% less damage taken and 6% protection from built in weapons. Treat that as unverified. Community

Specializations

Each Specialization is a row of hexagon cells. Some cells give a permanent stat, others hold a module you level by upgrading the cell. Unlocking every Basic cell opens the Active Module slot and a small stat boost. Then you pick one of the Additional rows to be active. Rows you do not select still keep their Permanent Effects, but the modules inside them switch off. Official

Leagues and live operations

Leagues

Trophies from wins move you up through the season: Recruit, Private, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Diamond, Expert, Master, Champion, with divisions inside each. Community

Tasks, events, leaderboards

Daily and weekly tasks pay currencies. Events run on timers with their own rewards. Leaderboards pay platinum and Upgrade Tokens by rank.

Store

Offers rotate on short timers. This account Of about 130 offers checked, most ran out in about 8 hours. The best value was Frozen Dagon MK2 with 6 weapons for $6. The worst was the $100 gold chip pack.

How the netcode changed

  1. Launch eraPhoton Cloud. Each client simulated its own robot and sent every move and shot as a separate RPC. Players reported network trouble and cheating was easy.
  2. Own serversA .NET server built on the Photon Server SDK on Windows Server, a master server and matchmaking servers. Basic damage checks and match results moved to the server.
  3. WorldStateA full redesign. The client now sends packets and gets game state back instead of RPC calls, over UDP with LiteNetLib. Lower server costs and fewer desyncs.
WorldState splits match data in three. RuleBook holds data that rarely changes during a match. ServerState holds the full world state the client has to draw. ClientState is what the player sends back. Official
Pixonic also built a shared backend called Singularity for login, user data, payments, A/B testing, analytics and an admin panel, plus its own Unity package manager and a ReactiveBindings library for MVVM UI.

Damage as a function Model

function hit(shooter, weapon, target) {
  let dmg = weapon.damage
  dmg *= shooter.pilotBonus(weapon) * shooter.abilityBonus()     // e.g. Overpower +20%
  dmg *= 1 + intelVsRobot(shooter.account, target.robot)          // your Intel on that robot
  dmg *= 1 - intelVsWeapon(target.account, weapon)               // their Intel on your gun
  dmg *= target.has("Deathmark") ? markBonus(weapon) : 1      // Pow, Boom +100%

  if (!weapon.ignoresShields) dmg = target.shields.absorb(dmg)  // Aegis, Absorber, Reflector
  const dp = target.dp * (1 - weapon.defenseMitigation)           // Reaper: mitigation = 1
  dmg *= 100 / (100 + dp)                                         // the DP curve
  target.durability -= dmg
}

The DP step uses the community formula. The order of the other steps is a guess from play, and Pixonic has not published it. It is here so a reader can see which systems touch a single shot.

Tactics

Built from your own match reports, not theory. The loadout is half of it. The other half is when you pull the trigger and where you are standing.

Platinum in Extermination needs you to lose nothing. You cannot drop a single robot. That one rule changes everything: survival outranks damage, and a tank that dies at 70% kills the run even if it out-damaged the floor. Build to finish with five robots alive, then worry about the kill count.

The check that never lies. Shoot something distant and watch its health bar. If a full magazine barely moves it, you are outside effective range no matter what the card said.

Close range, under 150m

  • Never walk in. Arrive. Samjok phase shifts, Scorpion and Noir Shenlou teleport, Retro Revenant blinks with full effect immunity, Baihu Lynx goes stealth. Crossing open ground on foot is the most common way a close robot dies.
  • The ability is for going in, not running out. Most players burn it escaping and end up in the open with no tool and no health.
  • Fight at corners, not in rooms. Sit behind cover at your range. They come around it already inside your kill zone.
  • Count your window before you commit. Samjok's phase shift, Revenant's 10 seconds of immunity, Shenlou's three blinks. If the target is not dead when it closes, you should already be leaving.

Mid range, 250m to 600m

  • Peek and reload. Empty the magazine, break line of sight while it refills, step back out. Standing through a reload is free damage for them.
  • Splash and homing do not need a clean line. Discipline, Smite, ULT Orkan, Pow and Boom all arc or track, so you can fire from behind partial cover. Beams cannot.
  • The job is usually interrupting, not killing. Damage on someone capping a beacon stops the capture. Half a magazine at 500m can beat a kill somewhere useless.

Long range, 800m and out

  • You do not start fights. You end them. Long guns lose duels. They punish someone already committed to your teammate.
  • Position to cover a chokepoint, not to have a view. The question is what you cover, not what you can see.
  • Your counter is a flanker. Void Shoggoth drops to half speed while Line Of Sight is up and takes 30% extra damage. That is exactly when a fast robot eats you, so do not raise the platform without a target.

How the three fit together

Beacon modes. Take the two nearest spawn first, free and fast. Mid robots hold the centre, the close robot flanks toward their spawn beacon, the long robot punishes whoever commits. Pulling one defender out of the centre fight usually decides it.

Never deploy your best robot first. Send something expendable, see what they actually brought, then answer it.

Titans are a timing tool, not a stat check. They charge over the match. Dropping one into a fight you are already losing throws it away. Drop it when it converts a beacon or breaks a stalemate.

How to judge a build

Five to ten matches, then decide. One bad match is noise.
Judge on damage dealt and beacons held, never kills. Kills get stolen constantly. Damage does not lie.
Change one thing at a time or you will not know what fixed it.
Two things that are easy to get wrong. Spare copies do nothing. War Robots does not merge weapons, so owning six Leinani just means six robots could carry one. And rarity is not power, level is. A level 1 orange loses to a level 8 grey today.

How the refresh works

A web page cannot reach another website on its own, so the button hands the job to a scheduled task on your account. The task reads warrobots.com, pulls apart the newest update notes, decides which changes touch robots and weapons you actually own, and writes them back here. It also runs by itself every Monday morning, so this stays current whether or not you press anything.

The test server is a separate install. WR Test is its own app on Google Play and through Apple TestFlight, on its own account. Nothing you do there touches this hangar.
